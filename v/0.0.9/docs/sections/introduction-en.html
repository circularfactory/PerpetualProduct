Introduction back to ToC

The transition from a linear to a circular economy requires product development approaches that simultaneously address evolving market needs and the integration of new technologies to establish value-adding products, while enabling cross-generational reuse of product substance to maximize value retention through integrated circular production patterns [1]. Within the Collaborative Research Centre 1574 “Circular Factory for the Perpetual Innovative Product”, decisions must be made about which reused components can be combined into which variant of the newest product generation while still meeting the required performance. Current semantic models in product development and systems engineering are mostly domain-specific and lack an integrated approach that unifies cross-generational product, behavior, reliability, and instance information [2]. This paper presents an integrated ontology that formalizes the cross‑generational description, planning, and modeling of perpetual innovative products. Grounded in the model of SGE – System Generation Engineering and the Product‑Production‑CoDesign approach, the ontology spans six fields of action: problem space modeling, requirements definition, product‑production integration, assessment of cross‑generational remanufacturing potential, interface management, and system generation traceability [3]. The system behavior domain, based on qualitative and quantitative modeling, describes unique relations between structure and behavior to enable instance‑specific assessment of functional requirements. Building on this, the reliability domain provides instance‑individual reliability prognoses over the intended service life by combining inspection data, material data, and historical usage profiles. The ontology is evaluated in participatory workshops with researchers and systems engineering experts and verified with an angle grinder case study, providing a model‑based framework that supports informed decision-making on cross-generational remanufacturing in the circular factory.
